# Plastic packaging rules and import

What EU law says about a plastic eyeshadow palette case, and what it takes to bring the cases from China into Poland. Researched on 2026-10-30 after a supplier asked "Isn't plastic banned in Europe?" ([[2026-10-30 EU plastic packaging and import research]]). This is a research summary from public sources, not legal or customs advice; the points marked "check" need the [[Accountant]], a customs agency or the [[Producer]]'s safety assessor.

## Short answer

- **Plastic cosmetic packaging is not banned in the EU.** A plastic palette case is fine to sell. No source found says otherwise.
- The rules that do apply are about **what the plastic is made of** (safety documents for the cosmetic safety report), **packaging law** (PPWR, and in Poland the BDO register) and, from 2030, **recyclability and recycled content**.
- Importing from China is normal: customs duty of about **5-6.5%**, **23% import VAT**, an EORI number, and in practice a customs agency.

## Is plastic banned? (EU rules)

**Single-Use Plastics Directive (2019/904).** Bans specific single-use items (cutlery, straws, cotton buds and the like) and requires marking on a few others such as wet wipes. A reusable palette case is not on any list.

**PPWR, the Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (EU) 2025/40.** Applies from **12 August 2026**. Packaging already on the market before that date is not affected. What it means for us:

- **Bans (Annex V, from 1 January 2030):** six formats, for example plastic shrink-wrap that groups items for convenience, and single-use toiletry miniatures in hotels. Eyeshadow palettes are not on the list. Avoid a plastic shrink-wrapped multi-pack from 2030.
- **Recyclability (from 1 January 2030):** packaging must reach at least grade C (≥70%) under design-for-recycling criteria; from 2038 at least grade B (≥80%). A case mixing ABS, a glued glass mirror and magnets may score badly; ask suppliers about this. Check: the detailed criteria per material come in later EU acts.
- **Recycled content (from 1 January 2030):** plastic packaging must contain a minimum share of recycled plastic. Packaging of cosmetics counts as "contact-sensitive", so for a non-PET plastic such as ABS the target is **10% in 2030 and 25% in 2040** (35% / 65% if the case were not contact-sensitive). The calculation method is still being set by the Commission. This is why our inquiry already asks for recycled-plastic options ([[Palette case inquiry email]]).
- **Is the case packaging at all?** PPWR says an item that is an integral part of the product and needed to contain it for its whole life is not packaging. A palette case that holds the pans until the palette is used up may fall under that. Not decided in the sources; check with the [[Producer]] or an advisor. The outer paper box and the shipping carton are packaging either way.
- **Minimisation (from 2030):** weight and volume reduced to the minimum; no double walls or false bottoms. E-commerce shipping boxes may have at most 50% empty space (method still to be set). Relevant to the [[Website]] shipping boxes.
- **Labels:** a harmonised material-composition label with pictograms, timing set by later EU acts (expected around 2028); the brand's name and postal address on the packaging.
- **Substances:** the old limit on lead, cadmium, mercury and hexavalent chromium in packaging stays (100 mg/kg in total, per the packaging industry source; check). The PFAS ban is for food-contact packaging only.
- **Who is responsible:** a company that sells packaging under its own name or brand counts as its "manufacturer" under PPWR, with a technical file and an EU declaration of conformity (self-assessed, no CE mark). So once our logo is on the case, that is probably us. Check.

**Cosmetics Regulation (EC) 1223/2009.** It does not ban any packaging material, but the safety report (CPSR, Annex I Part A) must cover the packaging: its purity and stability and anything that can migrate into the product. There is no fixed list of tests. The usual practice, which we should ask every case supplier for:

- Material specification (ABS grade, colourants, the mirror and any glue or paint).
- Declaration of compliance for the plastic raw materials, usually under the food-contact rules (Regulation 10/2011). Cosmetics do not legally need food-contact plastic, but safety assessors use it as the benchmark.
- Migration test results, if the assessor asks.
- REACH declaration: no Candidate List (SVHC) substances above 0.1% (REACH Article 33), and compliance with REACH Annex XVII restrictions.
- Heavy metals test (lead, cadmium, mercury, chromium VI).

The [[Producer]]'s safety assessor decides what is enough; see [[Compliance and CPNP]].

## Importing the cases from China to Poland

**Customs duty.** Depends on the CN code (classification), which is not settled:

| CN code | What it covers | Duty from China |
|---|---|---|
| 3923 10 90 | Boxes and cases of plastics for packing goods | 6.5% |
| 4202 12 50 | Vanity cases, powder boxes and similar containers with outer surface of moulded plastic | 5.2% |

A US customs ruling (1989) put an empty plastic compact with a mirror under "boxes and cases of plastics" (3923); in the EU a hinged, mirrored compact could also be read as a powder box (4202). Ask the customs agency; a binding tariff ruling (WIS) settles it if it matters. No EU anti-dumping duty on these goods was found (check in the EU trade defence database).

**Small parcels (samples).** Since 1 July 2026 the old duty-free limit for parcels under €150 is gone; until 1 July 2028 low-value postal and e-commerce parcels pay a flat €3 per item. How this applies to B2B sample parcels: check with the courier.

**Import VAT.** 23% (the Polish rate for these goods), charged on the customs value plus duty. Customs value normally includes freight and insurance up to the EU border, so CIF, not just the factory price. Check. Two ways to pay:

- At customs (on the customs declaration) and then deduct it in the VAT return, if we are an active VAT payer.
- Under **Article 33a of the VAT Act**: an active VAT payer can account for import VAT directly in the VAT return, so no cash leaves the company. Conditions: notify the customs and tax offices before the period starts (with certificates of no tax arrears) and clear goods through a customs representative.
- If we are **not** a VAT payer, import VAT cannot be deducted and is simply a cost. Whether we will be a VAT payer is still open: [[VAT and tax]].

**What we need.**

- **EORI number** for the company (free, from the customs office) before the first shipment. Needs a registered company first: [[Company registration]].
- **A customs agency** (agencja celna). It files the customs declaration, usually provides a guarantee so goods are released fast, and can be the representative for Article 33a. Freight forwarders often include it.
- **Documents from the supplier:** commercial invoice (seller, buyer, quantities, unit prices, currency, country of origin, Incoterms), packing list with net and gross weights, and the transport document (bill of lading for sea, air waybill for air). A certificate of origin is not needed for a non-preferential import from China.

**Incoterms.** Who pays freight, insurance, duty and VAT depends on the term in the quote. FOB / EXW: we organise shipping and clearance (cheapest, more work). DAP: the supplier delivers, we pay duty and VAT. DDP: the supplier pays everything (simplest, but then the supplier or its agent is the importer, the import VAT is hidden in the price, and we cannot deduct it). Several suppliers already quote by term ([[Sengmi Packaging]] DAP for samples, [[Walkin Printing]] DDP), so compare quotes on the same term. The Incoterms explanations above are general trade practice, not from the sources read.

**Rough landed cost.** Unit price + freight per unit + duty (5.2-6.5% of value with freight) + customs agency fee, plus 23% VAT that we get back only as a VAT payer. Feed this into [[Pricing and costs]].

## Poland: BDO register and packaging obligations

A company that sells products in packaging under its own brand is the "wprowadzający produkty w opakowaniach", even if production is outsourced (Packaging Act art. 8 pt 23a). Importing packaging also counts as placing it on the market. So we will probably need to:

- **Register in BDO** before we start selling (Waste Act art. 50). Fees: 200 zł for a micro-enterprise (800 zł otherwise), plus the same as a yearly fee from the second year.
- **Keep a record** of the packaging weight we put on the market each year (case, outer box, shipping cartons, fillers).
- **Report in BDO by 15 March** for the previous year.
- **Up to 1 tonne of packaging a year**, the recycling, product-fee and education-campaign duties do not apply, if de minimis aid papers are filed by 15 March. 1,000-5,000 palettes is far below 1 tonne. Registration and the report stay.
- Penalty for selling without BDO registration: 1,000 to 1,000,000 zł.
- Poland's law adapting to PPWR (new EPR rules) was not yet passed on 14 October 2026; until then the current rules apply.

Whether the [[Producer]] or we are the introducer depends on whose name is on the product; under the rule above it is us. Check with the [[Accountant]].
